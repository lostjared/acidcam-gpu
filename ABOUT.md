# 1 - Origin

ACMX was built around an idea that originally came to me through a meditation about fear and transformation. In the meditation, you first take something that troubles you and give it a visible form. You imagine the fear written on a large screen in front of you, where it is no longer only an invisible thought inside your mind. You observe it from a calm distance, transform it, clear the screen, and replace what was there with something different: love, calmness, and harmony. Finally, you imagine facing the thing again while remaining calm and in control. The important idea for me was not simply destroying an unpleasant image. It was taking something that had power over me, bringing it outside of myself, changing its form, and repeating that transformation until I could relate to it differently.

# 2 - Idea

That idea became the conceptual foundation of ACMX. The program takes an input and passes it through transformation functions. Technically, that input might be a photograph, a video, a live camera, or a 3D texture. Creatively, the input can also represent a thought, memory, mood, fear, or feeling. Instead of leaving that experience in its original form, ACMX gives me a way to put it onto the screen and manipulate it. Color can change. Shapes can dissolve. Images can mirror, fracture, stretch, glow, repeat, distort, blend with their own past, or become something completely abstract. The screen becomes a place where something difficult can be observed and changed rather than simply endured. This is the same basic process described elsewhere in the project.

# 3 - Connection

That is the deeper connection between the meditation and the software. ACMX is not just a collection of visual effects. At its core, it is a transformation system. Something enters in one state, a series of functions act upon it, and something different comes out. In programming terms that is an image-processing pipeline.
